Is the ORB edge worth real money — and whose money?

Own capital vs. prop-firm funding vs. buying richer data. Built on your 19,260-signal research dataset (Jun 2025–Jun 2026), 30k+ Monte Carlo runs, and primary-source research on 2026 prop-firm terms, algo policies, and market-data costs.

Verdict

The strategy is worth funding — through a static-drawdown CFD prop firm, not your own OANDA account, and only after a 2–3 month forward-test gate.

The Tokyo-session portfolio (XAU + NAS100, 1.5×ATR stop, 3:1, first-breakout, HTF-aligned) has a statistically real but modest edge: +0.31R/trade on XAU (95% CI [+0.04, +0.59]) and +0.22R on NAS100, with near-zero monthly correlation between the two. Conditional on that edge holding live, an FTMO-style $100k evaluation passes ~83–94% of the time at 0.5–0.75% risk with EV ≈ +$41–58k per attempt against a ~$540 fee (≈ $33–46k after a counterparty haircut), funded in ~2–3.5 months. Nothing else comes close: your own account earns the same edge on your own (smaller) capital at the most expensive venue tested, and the order-book-data path is a documented dead end for 5-minute alpha. The one honest blocker: this is a 12-month backtest that has never traded forward — the deployed demo bots run a different config (5:1 bracket). Close that gap first; it costs nothing but time.

04Path 3 — buy order-book data not for alpha

Your hypothesis ("beating PF 1.6 needs richer data") meets a hostile evidence base: order-flow predictability is concentrated at millisecond-to-seconds horizons, is dead by ~3 minutes (Cont/Cucuringu/Zhang 2023: negative OOS R² at 1-minute; Aït-Sahalia et al.: predictability gone by 3 min, 10ms of latency destroys 80% of it), and no disinterested study shows flow-confirmation improving breakout strategies — the one transparent NQ-ORB study found high-volume breakouts had worse follow-through. Your own CVD+ramp filter result (PF 1.07→1.37 in-sample) is worth a cheap falsification, not a subscription.

  • Rational spend: ~$150–400 one-off — Databento historical MBP-10/MBO for NQ+GC (starts against their $125 free credit). Pre-register the test: does delta-confirmation lift Config A out-of-sample? Accept the likely null.
  • Skip entirely: live depth feeds ($150–1,750/mo). No 5-minute strategy justifies them.
  • The real payoff of depth data is measurement, not alpha — queue/fill simulation to tighten your slippage estimates. Useful after you're funded, not before.
  • The literature's better-supported levers at your horizon are cross-asset/regime filters — which is what your HTF-trend and RVOL filters already are.

06What would change this answer

  • Forward test fails the gate (PF net < 1.2 or slippage > 0.05R worse than modeled) → stop; the whole EV chain is conditional on the edge being real. Don't rationalize a miss.
  • Config A's edge was 1-year-regime luck — 2026 was a strong gold-trend year and HTF-alignment rode it. A cheap check: re-run backtest_research.py on the older Feb 2025–Feb 2026 cache files already sitting in cache/ for a second, partially-independent window.
  • Firm rule drift — CFD prop terms change quarterly (~100 firms died 2024–25). Re-verify drawdown type, algo policy, and the FTMO Swing news rules the week you buy, not from this report.
  • Regulation — ESMA says retail prop isn't a priority (July 2026), but a UK/EU crackdown would compress the whole option. Another reason to withdraw aggressively.

07Methodology & honesty notes

  • All trade outcomes are your empirical per-trade R values (costs included per instrument), resampled by trading day; monthly-block bootstrap changed pass rates by <2pts for Config A (95.2% vs 96.9%), so serial correlation isn't hiding much — within this one year.
  • The deep risk is the sample, not the sampling: ~350 trades, one year, one market regime, zero live fills. Steps 1–2 exist to convert that from an assumption into a measurement.
  • The NAS100 Tokyo leg is statistically softer than XAU (P(no edge) 6.0% vs 1.2%) and was measured under rules originally selected on pooled data — not fully independent evidence. It earns its place through near-zero correlation and the same rule set, but it's the leg more likely to fail the forward gate; the plan degrades gracefully to XAU-only if it does. FTMO's US100 spread (~1.5–2.5pt) also runs wider than the 1.5pt modeled — the stressed column is the honest row for the NAS leg.
  • Funded-lifetime payouts assume the firm honors a 2-year horizon; haircut EVs 10–20% for denial/collapse risk (FTMO/Topstep have the strongest payment records; forfeiture-at-denial is the real mechanism, so bot rule-compliance must be airtight: visible SLs, <200 trades/day, no VPS where banned).
  • Prop pricing/rules were verified against primary firm pages where fetchable (July 5, 2026); flagged items (FTMO exact fee, FundedNext XAU leverage, E8 everything, Apex 4.0 pricing) came from secondary sources — re-verify at checkout.
  • Independent cross-check: an idealized-parameters simulation (35% WR, clean 3:1, no time-exits) gave 87.9% pass at 1% risk vs my empirical-distribution 78.7% — same conclusion, honest spread.
